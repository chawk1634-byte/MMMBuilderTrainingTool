Executive Summary

Our segmentation of 1,000 U.S. QSR consumers identifies five meaningfully distinct groups, differentiated by needs, behaviors, and attitudes toward the fast-casual dining category. Together, these segments explain why Shake Shack's customers visit, how often, and what it would take to deepen their relationship with the brand.

Key Findings

Methodology

This study employed a needs-based segmentation methodology — the standard approach for consumer research — which clusters respondents based on what they want from a category, rather than who they are demographically.
Sample Design

n=1,000 U.S. adults 18–54 who visited any QSR burger or fast-casual concept in the past 3 months. Nationally representative by region; weighted to Census targets for age, gender, and income.

Analytical Approach

28 attitudinal statements (5-pt Likert) were reduced to 5 orthogonal factors via principal component analysis. K-means clustering (k=5, 100 random seeds) segmented respondents into stable, differentiated groups.

Validation

Segment stability confirmed via hold-out sample (n=200). Silhouette score = 0.62 (good). Discriminant function correctly classified 88% of hold-out respondents to their primary segment.
