Key finding: The Familiarity-to-Consideration conversion (54% familiar, 31% considering) is the most significant funnel gap. Among guests who are familiar with the brand, only 57% actively consider it for their next visit. This is not an awareness problem — it is a relevance and occasion-fit problem. Increasing consideration among already-familiar guests is the highest-leverage brand investment available.

Competitive Position Summary
Where Shake Shack leads, matches, and trails the competitive set
Strategic read: Shake Shack leads the competitive average on Quality Perception (74% vs. 64% avg) and Differentiation (62% vs. 53% avg) — the two dimensions that justify premium pricing. Note: In-N-Out scores higher on both dimensions (78% quality, 64% differentiation), though its footprint is limited to the Western US. The critical vulnerability is Value Perception (38%), where Shake Shack trails all five competitors. This is not a quality problem; it is a price-framing and communication problem. Guests believe the food is better. They are not yet convinced it is worth the premium. That gap is addressable through messaging, portion framing, and value-tier menu architecture — not by lowering prices.

NPS and Value Perception by Segment
NPS (bars) and Value Perception score (line). Segments sorted by NPS.
The Value Skeptic gap: Value Skeptics show 44% unaided awareness — meaningfully above average — but only 22% consideration. That 22-point awareness-to-consideration gap is the largest in the portfolio and is driven almost entirely by Value Perception scores of 28%, the lowest of any segment. Value Skeptics know who Shake Shack is. They have decided it is not for them, primarily on price grounds. This is a communications challenge, not a product challenge — and it is different from the broader value problem because this segment specifically anchors on price-per-item comparisons rather than experience value. The intervention is not a discount; it is a reframe.
Section 05
Business Outcomes Model
Quantified linkages between brand metric improvements and downstream revenue impact. All assumptions are shown and can be challenged.
Sensitivity Table — Revenue Impact by Metric Improvement Size
Annual revenue impact ($M) at each improvement level. Assumes $12.50 average check, 4.2 annual visits for retained customer, 28M target population.
Key implication: A 5-point improvement in Consideration drives an estimated $6.0M in annual incremental revenue — more than a 10-point NPS improvement. This is because consideration operates at the top of the acquisition funnel, where the leverage on new trial is highest. Brands that underinvest in brand-building because it does not appear in attribution models are systematically ignoring the highest-ROI driver in this model.
